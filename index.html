Task Overview

You are on the information technology team of Pioneer University, a prestigious university in Hong Kong. You and your colleagues are going to hold a meeting to discuss the launch of the IT Gadgets Fair 2026. You should:

  • give your own suggestion(s) on the agenda items
  • explain the reason(s) for your suggestion(s)
  • listen actively and respond to others' suggestions by expressing preferences, agreement, or disagreement
  • reach a consensus on agenda items 2–4

As a group, you must cover all the agenda items below.

Meeting Details

Pioneer University Information Technology Team 16 December 20-- at 2:30 pm in the Meeting Room

Agenda

  1. Open the meeting
  2. Fair date
  3. Arrangements of the fair

    Possible arrangements:

    • On-campus venue
    • Types of gadgets exhibited
    • Special offers for students
    • Promotional gifts
  4. AOB

    Ways to inform students:

    • Mass student emails
    • SMS
  5. Close the meeting

Language Features

Numbered markers in the conversation show explanations of the use of language (modal verbs + bare infinitive, cohesive devices, contractions, suggestion patterns, etc.). A ✗ marks the incorrect form (e.g. can + bought), which should be the bare infinitive (e.g. can buy).

Communicative Functions & Useful Devices

Communicative functions (e.g. making suggestions, showing agreement, giving reasons) are highlighted in the italic blue line above each speaker's turn.

Cohesive devices / Linking words

  • Besides / Moreover / Furthermore
  • In addition / Additionally
  • Therefore / Thus / Hence / So
  • But / However

Contractions

  • I'm (I am) / It'll (It will)
  • We've (We have) / Let's (Let us)
  • That's (That is) / You're (You are)

Thinking words (air fillers)

Well…  Ummm…  uhhhh…

Modal verbs

Ability: can, could
Possibility: may, might
Permission: Can, May
Obligation: must, have to
Prohibition: Can't, Mustn't
Offers: Will, Would
Suggestions: Shall, Should
Requests: Could, Would
Future Possibility: Will, Shall
Past Probability: Could have, Would have

+ action verb (bare infinitive) + object (noun clause), e.g. Students can buy the gadgets at a cheaper price.

Grammar and Sentence Patterns

Modal verb + bare infinitive

✗ Students can surely bought / buying the gadgets at a cheaper price.
✓ Students can surely buy the gadgets at a cheaper price.
✗ Can we now decided / deciding on the fair date?
✓ Can we now decide on the fair date?

Making suggestions (action verbs: suggest + propose + recommend)

✗ I suggest / propose / recommend to watch a K-pop concert.
✓ I suggest / propose / recommend watching a K-pop concert.
✓ I recommend giving / that we (should) give special offers for the students.
✓ How about / What about holding the fair at the Campus Central Square?

Sentence structures

  • 1st conditional: If + S + V (present simple), S + will + bare infinitive (e.g. If a student obtains a GPA of 4 or above, they will receive a $200 cash coupon.)
  • Cause & effect: Since / Because / As + S + V + O, S + V + O.

Meeting Outcomes

📅 Fair Date Present mid-to-late August and December options to the management team
📍 Venue & Gadgets Hold the fair at Campus Central Square focusing on promoting tablets
🎁 Offers & Gifts GPA-based cash coupons ($200 for GPA ≥ 4); promotional gifts deferred to next meeting
📣 Informing Students (AOB) Mass student emails (2 weeks prior), class tutors' verbal announcements, and SMS messages
